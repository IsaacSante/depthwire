# What you need

The short version: a LiDAR iPhone, a Mac, something to hold the phone, and a projector.

## The essentials

| Thing | Notes |
|---|---|
| **iPhone with LiDAR** | iPhone 12 Pro or any later Pro model. This is your depth camera. |
| **Mac** | macOS 14 or later. It receives the depth and runs your visuals. |
| **Tripod or phone mount** | The phone must not move after you calibrate. A small tripod is enough. |
| **Projector** | Any projector works. Brighter is better in a room that isn't fully dark. |

That's all you need to start. Over Wi-Fi, nothing else is required.

## For long shows: go wired

Wi-Fi is fine for testing and short pieces. For an install that runs for hours, a cable is steadier, and it lets the phone charge the whole time.

This is what was used for the install this toolkit came from. Prices are what was paid in late 2026.

| Thing | What it's for | Where | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| **USB-C to Ethernet adapter** (Best Buy essentials) | Mac side: plugs into a spare USB-C port | Best Buy | about $15 |
| **Belkin Connect USB-C to Gigabit Ethernet + 100W Charging** (INC019BTBK) | Phone side: Ethernet in, and a charging port so the phone stays powered all night | B&H | $28.89 |
| **Pearstone Cat 6 patch cable, 100 ft** (CAT6-S100W) | Mac to phone, run along the walls | B&H | $22.95 |
| **Gaffer tape** | Taping the cable flat where people walk | | |

Why Ethernet and not a long USB-C cable: USB-C can't carry data reliably past about 13 ft. Ethernet runs 100 ft without trouble, and the cable is cheaper.

The phone-side adapter matters most. It needs a charging port as well as Ethernet, or the phone runs flat mid-show. Test charging and streaming together for an hour before you rely on it.

## The projector

The install used a used **Epson PowerLite E24**: 3,600 lumens, with a picture about 13'10" × 7'9" from roughly 19 ft back.

Two things worth knowing when you choose and place one:

- **Put it as far back as you can.** The picture gets bigger with distance, which is the reason for the long cable.
- **People cast shadows.** Anyone between the projector and the wall blocks the picture. Mount it high, or keep the audience behind a line on the floor.
